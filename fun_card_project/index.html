At the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), computational physics and coding act as the essential bridge between raw subatomic collisions and fundamental scientific discovery. With millions of proton-on-proton crashes occurring every second, detectors generate petabytes of complex data that cannot be manually processed. Physicists and computer scientists use object-oriented programming (such as C++ and Python) alongside specialized event generators like Pythia and Herwig to simulate particle trajectories and model theoretical outcomes. Furthermore, distributed systems like the Worldwide LHC Computing Grid (WLCG) and machine learning models—including graph neural networks—allow researchers to reconstruct particle tracks, filter out background noise, and hunt for rare phenomena like the Higgs boson
